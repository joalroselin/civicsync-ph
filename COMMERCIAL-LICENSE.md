1. **GNU Affero General Public License v3.0 or later (AGPL-3.0)**, free
   for everyone. You can use, modify, and even run CivicSync commercially,
   as long as you meet the AGPL's terms. The key one: if you run a modified
   version that others use over a network, you must make your complete
   source code available to those users under the AGPL.
2. **A commercial licence**, for organisations that can't or don't want to
   meet the AGPL's terms.

## Do I need a commercial licence?

You probably **don't** if you:

- use CivicSync as it is, or share your changes under the AGPL
- are an individual, community group, school, newsroom, or non-profit
  happy to publish your modifications

You probably **do** if you want to:

- build CivicSync's code into a closed-source or proprietary product
- run a modified version publicly without publishing your changes
- distribute it under terms other than the AGPL

## What a commercial licence gives you

- Use of the code without the AGPL's source-sharing obligations
- Terms agreed directly with the maintainer (scope, duration, and price
  depend on your use)
- A way to fund continued development of CivicSync

The licence covers **CivicSync's code only**. Legislative data belongs to
its sources (BetterGov Open Congress, BatasWatch, and the official Senate and
House records) and is subject to their terms.
